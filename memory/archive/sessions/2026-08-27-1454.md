On the v1 row-focus scope and the "existing interactive descendants must remain reachable" requirement - one concrete way to satisfy that without pulling in the full composite-entry pattern:

In a row-focus model, the row is the single tab stop and the in-cell widgets (sort headers, chevrons, selection checkboxes) drop out of the tab order. The APG composite-widget entry pattern (Enter/F2 to enter the row, Tab within, Escape to leave) is the full solution, but it's heavy for a v1. A lighter option that keeps the widgets reachable: keep the row as the tab stop, and let the widgets inside remain reachable via the screen reader's virtual cursor / browse mode even though they're not in the sequential tab order. That's the key distinction: "reachable" for AT doesn't have to mean "in the tab order" - browse mode can still get to them.

The trap to avoid is the middle ground: making the row focusable but leaving the widgets *also* in the tab order, which creates two competing tab stops per row and a confusing keyboard model. Either the row is the single stop (widgets reachable via browse mode / composite entry), or the widgets stay individually tabbable (which is the cell-nav model, out of v1). The one thing that doesn't work is both.

So for v1: row as the single tab stop, widgets reachable via browse mode, and document that composite entry (Enter/F2) is the follow-up if keyboard-only users need direct widget access. That keeps the v1 contract clean and the widgets reachable, without the full composite-entry machinery.
