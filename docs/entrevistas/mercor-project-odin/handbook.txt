Confidentiality
All Odin project information and materials are confidential. This includes the project
name, client identity, nature of the work and your involvement, along with this handbook,

Pay Policy
The hourly model began October 21, 2026 at 12:00 a.m. Pacific. Your contract will
determine your base rate.
For eligible hourly-paid tasks, time tracked within your stage allowance or weekly
limit is paid, including edits, send-backs and work on tasks that are not approved.
A task does not need to reach Ready for Delivery for that time to be paid. Idle or
off-project time, and hours above your limit, are not paid.
Only time tracked in Insightful can be paid; untracked time can’t be added later.
Time tracked while you were on a per-task contract is not paid hourly; that work is
paid per task.

Payment schedule
Payments for tasks that retain the old per-task terms follow the regular payment
schedule. Under the hourly model:
●​ Hourly pay is sent every Wednesday for the previous week (Saturday to
Friday, India time).

●​ Per-task pay is issued in a weekly batch.
●​ Tier bonuses are normally paid the Wednesday after the corresponding
hourly pay, but may arrive earlier, including with the hourly payment. Tier
bonuses are the amount paid above the base hourly rate in your contract.
●​ First payout: Verification may add 3-5 business days.
●​ Possible delays: Bank holidays, processing schedules, and bank cutoffs
may delay settlements.

Payment providers
●​ Stripe: The primary payment platform for ongoing contributors. After

●​ Wise: Generally used for one-time payments, contributors who have not

Pay questions
My contract shows $40/hour. How do tier bonuses affect my pay?
Your contract shows your base hourly rate before any tier bonus. In Production,
Writer adds 37.5% of your base rate and Super Writer adds 87.5%. With a
$40/hour base, that is $15 or $35 extra per eligible hour, for totals of $55 or $75. If
your current contract has a different base rate, apply the same percentages to
that rate.
Tier bonuses apply to eligible hours at your applicable tier within your weekly limit;
task approval is not required. Paying the additional amount as a bonus lets your
tier pay change without a new contract each time. This structure also applies if
your previous contract showed a higher rate.

Do I need to wait for an Insightful invitation before tracking paid work?
No. Once your contract is active, download the Workpuls desktop app from the
Insightful download page and sign in with Continue with SSO using your
@mercor.expert email. Select Odin and the Glowstone-Odin - Task timer for
eligible hourly-paid work. Follow Setup & access → Insightful for the full tracking
instructions.

Are my logged hours reviewed before payment?
Yes. Mercor reviews logged hours as part of each weekly pay cycle before
processing payment. Idle or off-project time may be removed. Log only eligible
active Odin work within your applicable allowance or cap. To challenge removed
hours, follow Disputes.

Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
It is really important on Project Odin to keep all personal information private, and
ensure nothing is leaked into the recording that shouldn’t be there.
Similarly, confidential project information must also not be shared in the recording.
⚠ PII must not leak into your tasks!

Tasks will never be approved if private or non-public personal information is included
in the recording. This video provides insight on where personal information sometimes
slips into recordings. Note: the video flags a Terminal username that isn't a full name.
A username is only PII if it shows your full name.

✅ Ok to include

❌ Not ok to include

Your @mercor.expert email and account name

Other real account or identification numbers

Public figures or public profiles

Mac addresses, or a Mac serial number + an IPv6
address

Mentioning Mercor or Project Odin aloud

Showing any Project Odin tools or documents

Obviously fake names, emails, and phone
numbers (John Smith, JohnSmith@example.com,
123-456-7890)

Personal emails, home addresses or phone
numbers of real people

Your first name

Personal photos or biometrics

There are several places PII can slip in, and recordings which include them will
need to be re-done. Pay particular attention to:
●​ App profile corners. Your name or photo in the top-right account area of
PowerPoint, Word, Google Docs, Sheets or Slides, or your browser's profile
button.
●​ Usernames in paths. Your full name in the Finder sidebar, a window title, a
save or export dialog (/Users/janesmith/...), or the Terminal prompt. A
first name or generic username (/Users/jane, /Users/recorder) is fine.

●​ Logged-in accounts. Your personal email or profile photo in Chrome, Gmail,
Google Drive or any web app.
●​ Autofill and history. Your name or email in search suggestions, recent-file
lists, or notification previews.

Workflow Guidelines
Each workflow you record should reflect realistic professional work for someone
in your professional domain. It should:
●​ Demonstrate meaningful expertise and judgment.
●​ Show the entire workflow to produce a clear final deliverable.
●​ Require the use of at least one app (ideally 2-3) which meaningfully
contributes to the output.
●​ Be mostly mouse-driven to assist with the recording and narrative.
●​ Take between 15:00 - 50:59 minutes to complete, including a short intro and
final epilogue summary.
●​ Have variety, where each task workflow you produce is meaningfully
different from the others you have done.

🎨
📊
🏢

Some quick workflow examples:
Healthcare / Medicine: Review de-identified patient education materials,
compare them with clinic guidance, and create an updated handout.
Engineering / Data: Clean and compare a permitted dataset, create clear
charts, and prepare a short findings report for a stakeholder.
Architecture: Update a room layout from a client brief, check the dimensions
and circulation, and export a labeled plan for review.
A strong recording shows how a professional thinks, makes decisions, and verifies
their work.
Do not pad the recording with unnecessary actions or waiting, and do not keep
tinkering after the task is complete. Check the output before you stop: it must be

accurate, readable, professionally formatted and usable. Minor writing errors are
acceptable only if they do not change the meaning or the result. Unless an
announced exception applies, the main app should do most of the work.

💡

Task complexity

Tasks may be simple, but must still be realistic, demonstrate your domain expertise and
fit naturally within 15:00–50:59; do not pad the recording.

Escalating a review
If you think a review decision on your task was wrong or unfair, you can escalate it
from the task page in Studio. Someone from the QC or Support team will check
your evidence and post a response on the task.
Escalating doesn't change your task's status. The task stays with you while the
escalation is open. An escalation is not a review, so it doesn't count toward your
3-review limit.
This is different from disputing an AutoQC finding. You dispute AutoQC findings
before you submit; you escalate a reviewer's decision after your task comes
back to you.

When you can escalate
You can escalate when your task is back with you after a review or QC send-back.
That means it's in In Progress or Needs QC Revision.
Only escalate a review that has actually happened. If you escalate a first draft that
hasn't been reviewed yet, it will be closed as invalid.​
​

Only escalate when you have a real, evidence-based reason to think the review
was wrong. Repeatedly escalating with false or misleading information may
affect your standing on the project.

How to escalate
1.​ On the task page, scroll to the end of Creating your task. Below Before you
submit you'll find the Review escalation section.
2.​ In Why are you escalating?, say which review decision you disagree with
and why. Be specific and include timestamps or links as evidence.
3.​ Tick Escalate this review. You can't tick it until you've written your note.
4.​ Stop there and don't resubmit. Wait for a response.
Example note: "The reviewer failed check 2 for window overlap, but the only
overlap is at 04:12–04:15, which is 3 seconds…"

While your escalation is open
●​ Your note is locked once the Escalate this review box is ticked.
●​ To edit your note or withdraw the escalation, untick the box. To escalate
again, edit the note and tick the box again.

When you get a response
The response appears in Escalation resolution on your task, and the Escalate this
review box will be unticked. It starts with one of these outcomes:
●​ Upheld: the original review stands. Make the edits you were asked for, then
resubmit.
●​ Overturned: the review decision was wrong. Resubmit as the resolution
tells you, often unchanged. The next reviewer will see the resolution.
●​ Invalid: there was nothing to escalate, e.g. the task hadn't been reviewed
yet. Follow the instructions in the resolution.
You must resubmit your task yourself in every case. A resolution doesn't move
your task forward on its own.
If you still disagree after the response, you can edit your note and tick Escalate
this review again.

Review Limits
Each task has a maximum of 3 total reviews.
●​ This includes the initial submission, which will be review 1.
●​ You can revise and resubmit up to 2 additional times for review.
●​ After review 3, if the task is not approved, it will be rejected and discarded.
●​ If you have a task in progress with 3 or more reviews, your final revision
must be submitted by Friday, October 30 at 11:59 p.m. PT for its final
review.
○​ If further changes are needed, it will be discarded.
○​ The 3-review limit is effective October 29, 2026 at 5:30 p.m. PT. A
task already at or beyond 4 reviews still receives its final review if it is
waiting in the queue after the deadline. You need to submit by the
deadline; you do not need approval by then.
⚠ Important

Address all feedback before resubmitting. Each resubmission counts as another
review attempt, so do not submit a partial fix while leaving other issues unresolved. If
feedback is unclear, bring your task link and specific questions to the 24/7 Writer Q&A
before re-recording.

RECORD · 15:00–50:59, incl. intro and epilogue

Narration habits

●​ Don't rush. As one top writer put it, "There is no
harm in taking 5 seconds to organize your
thoughts."
●​ Treat narration as part of the task. Narration is
where most early send-backs come from, so it isn't
an extra.
●​ Assume the listener can't see your screen.
Remember the Eyes-Closed Test as you explain the
what, where and why of each step.
●​ Click with purpose. Narrate every click and scroll,
and use your cursor movement as the cue to speak.
●​ Watch the clock. If you are nearing the 50-minute
mark, keep narrating every step but be intentional
about speaking concisely to stay within the
recording limit.
●​ Name files as you go. Save outputs as, for example,
"final output 1" to avoid having to rename them later.
●​ Keep the intro short. In under 20 seconds, say only
"I will be beginning my task soon. Here is the
workflow I have in mind." Do not read the Goal
aloud.
●​ Keep the epilogue short. Aim for 3–4 minutes (4:00
maximum).

FAQ

What if something goes wrong during the recording?
●​ If it is a significant mistake that alters the final output, stop and restart with a fresh attempt.
●​ Small slips are normal. Continue recording but address the situation. Say what you are fixing, fix it, check
the fix, and carry on. For example, if a menu option has moved, narrate that and show the alternative.
Mention this mistake again in the epilogue.

10/28 Tip: 5 tips to avoid re-recording your task!
Most re-records come down to a handful of avoidable issues. These tips come from writers who
now get tasks approved on first submission.
A few minutes of prep at each stage can save you from recording the whole task again.
1. Keep the Goal identical in all three places
●​ Copy only the Goal section from Studio, exactly as written, into (1) the Task Description
in Task Recorder, (2) your opening Goal document, and (3) the Request in Metadata.rtf.
●​ No Approval ID, no edits, no leftover text from your last task.
●​ Check for wording differences before recording. If the wrong Goal appears in the
recording, the recording needs correcting. If only the Request in Metadata.rtf is wrong,
correct the file and replace it in Metadata.rtf Upload; that file-only issue does not require
re-recording.
2. Run a PII check before you press record
●​ Close every window you aren't using for this task, including Finder or File Explorer
windows behind your main app.
●​ Keep your desktop free of personal files. Check your Downloads or Recents folders if
Finder will be displayed during recording.
●​ Consider screenshotting each app screen you'll use and ask Gemini to flag anything
personal
Copy-pasteable prompt: You are a PII auditor. Before I record, check this
screenshot for anything that could identify me or another real person:
names, email addresses, usernames, profile pictures or initials, file

chat or notification previews, background windows, desktop icons and
menu bar items. Ignore Project Odin, Studio and Mercor references and
approved test accounts.

matching numbered list saying what each item is, where it is and how

3. Stick to apps you already use, and reuse them
●​ Build tasks around 2 or 3 apps you work in regularly (check they're not on the Restricted
App List).

●​ Once those apps are configured to be PII compliant, future tasks that utilize them will

4. Narrate the what and the where for every click
●​ Assume the listener can't see your screen. Say what you're clicking, where it is, and why
that step matters.
●​ The "where" is the part most often missed. Slow down, use your cursor movement as the

●​ If something goes wrong mid-recording, say what happened and what you're trying

5. Check length and files before you upload
●​ Your recording must be between 15:00 and 50:59 including intro and epilogue, so keep a
visible timer running off-screen.

Narration checks (what you said and did)
AutoQC may miss evidence shown in your recording. Narration coverage counts actions that
change the document or file: editing, formatting, moving content, saving or exporting.

Writer FAQ
1.​General Understanding
What am I actually doing in this project?
You plan, record, validate and submit realistic professional workflows that someone in your field
would perform, on your Mac. Each recording shows the complete workflow, narrated, and ends
with a saved deliverable.

Am I being evaluated on how well I do the task?
Yes. Quality is essential, and the task itself must be distinct, realistic and within your domain of
expertise. It should demonstrate authentic professional expertise through deliberate execution,
clear narration and a complete deliverable. Ask yourself: "Can your friend with no domain
expertise complete this task?" If yes, choose a task that better uses your domain skills. Review
05. Task Guidelines and Common mistakes, and tag @Maven in #odin--ideation if you are
unsure.

Which version of the guidelines applies to my task?
The guidelines in effect when you submit. If guidance changes while you're recording, you get a
short allowance of one to two hours. This doesn't apply to tasks you've already submitted.
Changes are announced in #odin--announcements and logged in the 02. Changelog. Earlier
onboarding offers keep their original amounts, eligibility, deadlines and permitted bonus
stacking.

3.​Tasks
How does app reimbursement work?
The $50 USD cap is shared across all apps per writer per rolling 30 days, including tax; there
are no exceptions. Ordinary eligible purchases do not need pre-purchase sign-off. Claim with
the receipt after you submit a complete task that meaningfully uses the app; that task does not
need to be approved first, but you must already have at least one approved task. Purchases
made before the policy was announced are not eligible. Annual or lifetime plans and bundles
need separate approval and still count toward the cap. You may use your own licensed copy if it
was activated before recording and no name, email or license detail appears on screen.

Do the new app restrictions apply to a task I already started?
The app restrictions apply to new work started on or after October 16, 2026 at 18:08 PDT. Work
already underway before then is protected; it will not be discarded, restarted or rejected only
because of the update. Ideas or tasks previously rejected for an app that is now allowed may be

reconsidered, subject to all other checks. If an older Studio label or a copied list disagrees, use
the Restricted apps tab in this handbook. Banned apps must not be opened, shown or used in
new recordings.

What happens when I reach my hours or open-task limit?
Onboarding has three paid setup hours total; you may finish the first task afterward under its
fixed-payment terms. For writers in Onboarding, the first task reaching Review Approved
unlocks Trial’s initial 12-hour allowance. Wait until the allowance appears in Insightful before
starting the next hourly-paid task. After three Trial tasks reach Review Approved, you enter
Production as a Writer; rejected tasks do not count toward those three approvals.
During Trial, each qualifying task adds three paid hours to your allowance. Review Approved,
QC Approved, RFD - Minor (Quality Review A) and Ready for Delivery qualify, including earlier
tasks. Each task counts once, even if it passes through multiple qualifying statuses; hours
update automatically. You do not need to wait for RFD solely to earn hours. Your first-task
approval payment follows your onboarding offer and the approval/pay-run conditions below. Tier
efficiency still uses Ready for Delivery.
If Trial genuinely reaches zero, finish the current task without further hourly pay and wait for
earned hours before resuming hourly-paid work. Production weekly caps are firm: 40 hours for
Throttled Writer, 60 for Writer and 80 for Super Writer. You can check your weekly cap in your

Why does my task show as approved by me?
Automatic approval can be logged under the submitter’s name. This does not mean you
reviewed your own task. It can still be selected for final QC.

Can I claim a task marked Unclaimed or Available to claim?
No. Please do not claim tasks marked Unclaimed or Available to claim. Create and submit your
own idea instead: open Studio, select Odin Production Full Workflow, then Create Task.

Are tasks reviewed on weekends?
Yes. The reviewer team spans time zones, so tasks are picked up throughout the week.
Turnaround depends on queue depth rather than the day, though it can be slower at weekends,
on holidays and at quieter hours. There is no guaranteed order, so a newer task can be picked
up before an older one. Ready for Review means it is queued and there is nothing for you to do.

Can I change my idea after it is approved?
Once an idea is approved, its description and its app list are both locked. If your plan changes
materially, select Revise idea: the task returns to Idea Returned, where you can update
Describe your idea and Apps before selecting Resubmit idea, and you should wait for the new
approval before you record. Revise idea is only available in Odin Production Full Workflow. You
can find the ‘Revise Idea’ button in the left-side pane on Studio - please scroll down to see it. A
greyed-out card headed “Your original idea” is there for reference only and does not affect your
review or QC result. Always record against the newest approved revision. Do not create a
duplicate task.
A pending change request does not replace the existing approval. If the revised idea is flagged
against your own earlier task, share both links and the exact flag in #odin--ideation and have the
comparison checked before creating another copy. Rejection alone does not guarantee that a
resubmission avoids duplicate detection. Use #odin--helpdesk for a submitted-task technical
block.

How should I label and package my deliverable files?
Include original starting files as input 1, input 2, and so on; downloaded or intermediate files as
artifact 1, artifact 2; and the finished products as final output 1, final output 2, with their proper
extensions. Websites visited only for research or context are not input files and do not need to
be packaged.

Do I need an input file if I create the task from scratch?
No. A task created from scratch does not need an input file. Deliverables.zip must include any
actual starting files used, intermediate artifacts and at least one clear final output, correctly
labeled and readable.

What if the app cannot produce a normal deliverable file?
When the app cannot produce a normal deliverable file, a screenshot or another readable
output format can be acceptable. Include evidence of the completed form or submission and
any actual starting files used.

No. VPN use while working on Odin is strictly prohibited. If a VPN is detected while you are
