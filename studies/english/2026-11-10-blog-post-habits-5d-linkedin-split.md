## Tramo 1 (632 caracteres)

Two years of my career fit in one sentence I said in every interview: "I read and write in English all day, but I don't speak it well." I said it like a small disclaimer. It was the most expensive sentence I know.

Two jobs I really wanted ended the same way. The technical interviews went well, and then someone from the client asked how comfortable I was leading a call in English. Nobody was unfair. They needed what they needed. I had been treating my spoken English as a detail of my profile, when it was actually the door.

So I made a change. Not a dramatic one - nobody should expect a montage - but a change I can point to.

---

## Tramo 2 (627 caracteres)

The first thing I did was stop calling it a weakness and start calling it a task. Once I booked a real course - twice a week, with a teacher who corrects me out loud - it stopped being a feeling and became a calendar item.

The second thing was less flattering. I signed up for the wrong level. I ended up in a B1+ group by mistake, and the first class was comfortable, almost flattering. But comfortable was the exact problem I was trying to solve, so I asked to be moved, and I landed in the B2+ group where I belong.

I'd love to tell you I went back one level on purpose. I didn't. I just refused to stay where it was easy.

---

## Tramo 3 (629 caracteres)

Now for the honest part. I'm four classes in, and my voice is still not where I want it. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

The point was never the grammar - my written English is fine - it's the speed: saying a sentence before my brain has time to reconsider it. And I've improved at that, mostly by making peace with being messy in public.

If you are stuck in a similar place, here is what I would say.

Start with the honest version of the goal. "Improve my English" is not a goal, which is why it survives for a decade without changing anything. "Handle a thirty-minute call without asking people to repeat themselves" is.

---

## Tramo 4 (496 caracteres)

Don't be a creature of habit about your comfort zone. I know how tempting it is to stay in a group where you already understand everything. Choose the level where you have to work, not the one where you feel clever.

Let people hear you before you are ready. You shouldn't wait for perfect sentences, because waiting is just practising silence. Say the half-formed thing, correct yourself out loud, and let people wait two seconds. It's a skill, and it reads as confidence to everyone except you.

---

## Tramo 5 (527 caracteres)

Finally: name it instead of hiding it. Telling an interviewer that my written English was stronger than my spoken English was the scariest thing I did this year, and it changed the conversation completely.

I spent two years being quietly afraid of that sentence. It's one sentence long.

If I could leave you with one idea: you shouldn't wait for your English to become a strength before you start using it. It only becomes a strength after you use it, and that order doesn't reverse.
