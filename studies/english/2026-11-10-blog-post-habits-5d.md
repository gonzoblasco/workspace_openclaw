# Unit 5D - Writing: a blog post about habits (Speakout 3rd B2+)

**Tarea:** Write a blog post about your experience of making a change in your life. Give some advice to others. Use phrases for giving advice.
**Modelo del libro:** "Stuck in a rut?" (p. 65) - gancho con pregunta directa, rutina descrita con listas de tres, decisión, cambios.
**Recursos del libro a usar:** phrasal/idiomatic set de 5D (creature of habit, plan ahead, get stuck in a rut, sense of comfort, step outside your comfort zone); Key phrases de discusión; Writing Bank p. 108.
**Registro:** blog post - primera persona, contracciones, una pregunta al lector, tono de conversación.
**Versión vigente:** apertura literal (pregunta directa al lector, como el modelo del libro) + cierre en condicional. Elegida por Gonzo el 2026-11-10. **Destino doble: tarea del campus + post de LinkedIn.**

## Why I Stopped Waiting to Be Fluent

Have you ever said *"I read and write in English all day, but I don't speak it well"* and felt almost proud of the honesty? I used to say exactly that. I meant it as a small disclaimer. It turned out to be the most expensive sentence I know.

Two jobs I really wanted ended the same way. The technical interviews went well, and then someone from the client asked, in a friendly tone, how comfortable I was leading a call in English. I said I was B2 and that written English was my strongest channel, and the process quietly stopped there. Nobody was being unfair. They needed what they needed. But I had been treating my spoken English as a detail of my profile, when it was actually the door.

So I made a change. Not a dramatic one - nobody should expect a montage - but a change I can point to.

The first thing I did was stop calling it a weakness and start calling it a task. For years I had carried it around like a personality trait, something about me that wasn't quite good enough. Once I booked a real course - twice a week, with a teacher who corrects me out loud - it stopped being a feeling and became a calendar item. That shift did more than any study method.

The second thing was less flattering. I signed up for the wrong level. When I registered, I ended up in a B1+ group by mistake, and the first class was comfortable, almost flattering. But comfortable was the exact problem I was trying to solve, so I asked to be moved, and after that mess I landed in the B2+ group where my CV says I belong. I'd love to tell you I went back one level on purpose. I didn't. I just refused to stay where it was easy.

Now for the honest part. I'm four classes in, and my voice is still not where I want it. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. The point was never the grammar - my written English is fine - it's the speed: saying a sentence before my brain has time to reconsider it. And I've already improved at that, mostly by making peace with being messy in public.

If you are stuck in a similar place, here is what I would say.

Start with the honest version of the goal. "Improve my English" is not a goal, which is why it survives for a decade without changing anything. "Handle a thirty-minute project call without asking people to repeat themselves" is. You'd better not book a course until you can picture the scene you are preparing for.

Don't be a creature of habit about your comfort zone. I know how tempting it is to stay in a group where you already understand everything. You should choose the level where you have to work, not the one where you feel clever. If a class feels easy for a whole month, you probably took the easy way out.

Let people hear you before you are ready. You shouldn't wait for perfect sentences, because waiting is just practising silence. Say the half-formed thing, correct yourself out loud if you need to, and let people wait two seconds. Correcting yourself out loud is a skill, and it reads as confidence to everyone except you.

Finally, name it instead of hiding it. Telling an interviewer that my written English was stronger than my spoken English was the scariest thing I did this year, and it changed the conversation completely. I spent two years being quietly afraid of that sentence. It's one sentence long.

If I could leave you with one idea, it would be this: you shouldn't wait for your English to become a strength before you start using it. It only becomes a strength after you use it, and that order doesn't reverse, however long you wait.

## Notas de versión (por qué esta y no la anterior)

1. **El error de nivel es el dato real.** Versión previa: "elegí un nivel de repaso a propósito". Gonzo: arrancó en B1+ por error de inscripción y pidió el pase a B2+. Mantener la versión anterior era un hecho inventado, y encima contradecía el registro del plan (`studies/english/README.md`, corrección del 27/10). El error además funciona mejor narrativamente: *"I'd love to tell you I went back one level on purpose. I didn't."*
2. **"Two months in" era falso.** Son 4 clases (la 5ª cae en feriado). Corregido a "I'm four classes in".
3. **Se sumó el léxico de la unidad** (creature of habit, comfort zone, taking the easy way out) porque la consigna viene de 5D y el profe evalúa el léxico esperado del nivel.
4. **Frases para dar consejo, explícitas:** `here is what I would say` · `You'd better not…` · `You should choose…` · `You shouldn't wait for…` · `I would say` / `If I could leave you with one idea, it would be…` · `Don't be a creature of habit about…`
5. **Gramática para el criterio de nivel:** `I had been treating` (past perfect continuous), `Once I booked` (once + past), mixed conditionals implícitos (`If a class feels easy… you probably took`), `I'd love to tell you… I didn't`, `It's one sentence long` (short-form emphasis), modales para consejo.
6. **Cohesión:** `Have you ever… → So I made a change → The first thing I did → The second thing → Now for the honest part → If you are stuck → Start with → Don't be → Let people → Finally → If I could leave you with one idea`.
7. **Apertura literal (2026-11-10).** Gonzo eligió la pregunta directa porque es la que hace el modelo del libro (*"Have you ever felt that your life was just one long, endless routine?"*): interpela al lector y recién después se pone de ejemplo ("I used to say exactly that"). El cierre quedó en condicional para sumar gramática de nivel y porque la pregunta final sonaba menos a cierre de blog.
8. **Doble destino.** El texto se entrega al campus y además se publica en LinkedIn. Por eso evitó nombres de empresas y reclutadoras: "two jobs I really wanted" y "someone from the client" alcanzan, y el texto no queda atado a un proceso puntual en una superficie pública.
9. **Longitud aceptable para LinkedIn:** ~650 palabras es largo para el feed pero funciona con "ver más". Si prefiere un post corto (150-250 palabras) con link al blog, se deriva una versión corta.
